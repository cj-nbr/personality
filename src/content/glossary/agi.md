## Definition

Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) is your gross income minus specific adjustments. In the United States, AGI is a key figure that determines eligibility for many tax deductions and credits.

## Explanation

AGI is calculated as:

```
AGI = Gross Income − Above-the-Line Deductions
```

"Above-the-line" deductions are those that reduce your gross income directly, such as contributions to a traditional IRA, student loan interest, and self-employment tax.

## Examples

- A taxpayer with $60,000 in gross income and $5,000 in above-the-line deductions has an AGI of $55,000.
- AGI may limit the amount of itemized deductions you can claim — higher AGI can reduce or eliminate certain deductions.
- Many tax credits (such as the Earned Income Tax Credit) have AGI-based phaseout thresholds.
