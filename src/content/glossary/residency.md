## Definition

Residency, in tax context, refers to the legal status that determines which country has the right to tax an individual's worldwide income. Tax residency is distinct from immigration status and citizenship.

## Explanation

Tax residency is determined by a set of rules that may include physical presence, permanent home availability, center of vital interests, and habitual residence. Different countries apply different tests, and some individuals may be tax residents of more than one country simultaneously.

## Types of Residency

### Fiscal (Tax) Residency
Determines where you owe taxes. May differ from where you physically live.

### Immigration Residency
Determines your legal right to live and work in a country. Does not directly determine tax obligations.

### De Facto Residency
Based on where you actually live and maintain your center of economic interests.

## Examples

- An American working abroad may still be a U.S. tax resident because the U.S. taxes citizens regardless of where they live.
- A digital nomad who spends 183 days in a country in a single tax year may become that country's tax resident.

## Why It Matters

- Determines your worldwide tax obligations
- Affects filing requirements
- Influences the tax treaties and foreign tax credits available to you
