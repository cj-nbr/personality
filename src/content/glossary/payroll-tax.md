## Definition

Payroll tax is a tax levied on employee wages and salaries, shared between employers and employees in most jurisdictions. Payroll taxes fund specific programs such as Social Security and Medicare.

## Explanation

Payroll taxes differ from income taxes in that they are typically:
- **Flat-rate** (rather than progressive) up to a wage base limit
- **Shared** between employer and employee
- **Dedicated** to specific programs rather than general revenue

## Examples

- In the United States, the employee pays 6.2% for Social Security (up to the wage base) and 1.45% for Medicare. The employer matches these amounts.
- Self-employed individuals must pay both the employee and employer portions, known as Self-Employment Tax.
