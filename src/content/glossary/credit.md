## Definition

A tax credit is a dollar-for-dollar reduction of your actual tax liability. Unlike a deduction, which reduces taxable income, a credit reduces the amount of tax you owe directly.

## Explanation

Credits are generally more valuable than deductions because they reduce your tax bill at full value, regardless of your marginal tax rate. A $1,000 credit saves you $1,000 in taxes, whereas a $1,000 deduction saves you less if your marginal rate is below 100%.

### Refundable vs. Non-Refundable

- **Refundable credits** can result in a refund even if your tax liability is zero. Example: Earned Income Tax Credit.
- **Non-refundable credits** can reduce your tax to zero but cannot generate a refund.

## Examples

- A $2,000 non-refundable credit reduces a $5,000 tax bill to $3,000.
- A $2,000 refundable credit applied to a $1,000 tax bill results in a $1,000 refund.

## Common Credits

- Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)
- Child Tax Credit
- Education Credits (American Opportunity, Lifetime Learning)
- Foreign Tax Credit
- Saver's Credit

## Summary

Credits are dollar-for-dollar reductions of your tax bill and are generally more valuable than deductions. Refundable credits are the most valuable type.
