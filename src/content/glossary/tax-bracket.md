## Definition

A tax bracket is a range of income that is taxed at a specific rate in a progressive tax system. Each bracket has a lower and upper threshold, and income within that range is taxed at the corresponding rate.

## Explanation

In a progressive tax system, tax brackets ensure that higher-income earners pay a higher percentage of their income in taxes. However, only the income within each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate — not all of your income.

For example, if the 22% bracket applies to income from $45,001 to $90,000, the first $45,000 is taxed at the lower bracket rates, and only the portion above $45,000 up to $90,000 is taxed at 22%.

## Examples

- U.S. 2023 Federal Tax Brackets (Single Filer):
  - 10%: $0 – $11,000
  - 12%: $11,001 – $44,725
  - 22%: $44,726 – $95,375
  - 24%: $95,376 – $182,100
  - 32%: $182,101 – $231,250
  - 35%: $231,251 – $578,125
  - 37%: $578,126 and above

## Common Misconceptions

Moving into a higher bracket does NOT mean all your income is taxed at the higher rate. The progressive system applies higher rates only to the portion of income above each threshold.
