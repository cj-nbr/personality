## Definition

The marginal tax rate is the tax rate applied to your **last dollar** of income — or equivalently, the rate that applies to the next dollar you earn. In a progressive tax system, your marginal rate equals the rate of the highest tax bracket your income falls into.

## Explanation

Your marginal tax rate is useful for understanding the tax impact of incremental income or deductions:

- Earning one more dollar pushes that dollar into your marginal bracket
- A deduction saves you money at your marginal rate

This is why knowing your marginal rate helps with financial decisions — it tells you the real value of additional deductions or income.

## Examples

- If you earn $100,000 and your highest bracket is 24%, your marginal tax rate is 24%. A $1,000 deduction saves you $240 in taxes.
- If you are in the 12% bracket, a $500 deduction saves you $60 at the margin.
