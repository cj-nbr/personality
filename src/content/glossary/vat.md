## Definition

Value-Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax applied to the value added to goods and services at each stage of production and distribution. VAT is used widely in Europe and many other countries around the world.

## Explanation

VAT works by taxing the "value added" at each step of the supply chain. Each business in the chain charges VAT on sales and can reclaim VAT paid on purchases, passing only the net amount to the government.

## Examples

- A manufacturer sells raw materials to a factory and charges 20% VAT. The factory pays the VAT to the government but can reclaim the VAT it paid when purchasing the materials.
- The European Union operates a VAT system with standard rates ranging from 17% to 27% depending on the member state.
