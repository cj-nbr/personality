## Definition

Withholding is the amount of an employee's wages that an employer withholds (deducts) from each paycheck and remits directly to the government as an advance payment toward the employee's annual tax liability.

## Explanation

Withholding ensures that taxpayers pay their taxes throughout the year rather than in a single lump sum at filing time. Employers use the employee's W-4 form (in the U.S.) or equivalent form to determine the withholding amount based on the employee's anticipated tax situation.

## Examples

- An employee earns $4,000 per month and has $800 withheld for income tax. Over the year, $9,600 is withheld as an advance on their tax liability.
- If too little is withheld, the employee may owe money at filing with potential penalties. If too much is withheld, the employee receives a refund.

## Tips

- Review your withholding when your personal or financial situation changes (marriage, new job, additional income sources).
- Use a withholding calculator to ensure the right amount is being deducted.
