## Definition

Taxable income is the portion of your gross income that is subject to income tax after all deductions, exemptions, and adjustments have been applied. It is the figure used to calculate your actual tax liability.

## Explanation

Taxable income is calculated as:

```
Taxable Income = Gross Income − Deductions − Exemptions
```

In many modern tax systems (including the United States after the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act), personal exemptions are no longer available, so taxable income is primarily determined by:

```
Taxable Income = Gross Income − Deductions
```

The deduction can be either the **standard deduction** (a flat amount set by law) or **itemized deductions** (individual qualifying expenses), whichever is greater.

## Examples

- Gross income of $80,000 minus the standard deduction of $13,850 (2023 U.S. figure) = taxable income of $66,150.
- Gross income of $100,000 minus $20,000 in itemized deductions = taxable income of $80,000.

## Tips

- Keep records of deductible expenses throughout the year.
- Compare the standard deduction to your potential itemized deductions each year — the standard deduction amount changes annually.
