## Definition

A deduction is an amount that reduces your taxable income, thereby lowering your overall tax bill. Deductions are one of the primary mechanisms through which the tax code reduces the burden on taxpayers.

## Explanation

Deductions work by lowering the amount of income that is subject to tax. The value of a deduction depends on your marginal tax rate — the higher your bracket, the more valuable each dollar of deduction.

### Above-the-Line vs. Below-the-Line

- **Above-the-line deductions** reduce your gross income directly and are available regardless of whether you itemize. Examples include IRA contributions, student loan interest, and self-employment tax deductions.
- **Below-the-line deductions** reduce your taxable income after adjusted gross income is calculated. These include the standard deduction and itemized deductions.

## Examples

- A $2,000 deduction at a 22% marginal rate saves you $440 in taxes.
- The standard deduction for single filers in the U.S. was $13,850 for the 2023 tax year.
- Charitable donations, mortgage interest, and state taxes are common itemized deductions.

## Tips

- Keep receipts and records of deductible expenses throughout the year.
- Compare the standard deduction to potential itemized deductions annually — the standard deduction amount adjusts for inflation each year.
