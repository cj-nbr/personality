## What Is a Tax Credit?

A tax credit is a dollar-for-dollar reduction of your **tax liability**. Unlike a deduction, which reduces taxable income, a credit reduces the actual amount of tax you owe.

## Deduction vs Credit

| Aspect | Deduction | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Reduces | Taxable income | Tax bill |
| Value | Depends on your marginal rate | Same value for everyone |
| Example | $1,000 deduction at 22% = $220 saved | $1,000 credit = $1,000 saved |

**A $1,000 credit is almost always more valuable than a $1,000 deduction** — unless you are in a very low bracket.

## Types of Tax Credits

### Refundable Credits
A refundable credit can reduce your tax liability below zero, resulting in a **refund** to you. Even if you owe no tax, you may still receive the full or partial credit.

**Common refundable credits:**
- Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)
- Child Tax Credit (partially refundable)
- Premium Tax Credit (health insurance marketplace)

### Non-Refundable Credits
A non-refundable credit can reduce your tax to zero, but any excess is **not** refunded.

**Common non-refundable credits:**
- Child and Dependent Care Credit
- Education Credits (American Opportunity, Lifetime Learning)
- Saver's Credit (retirement savings contributions)
- Foreign Tax Credit

### Partially Refundable Credits
Some credits are refundable up to a certain limit. For example, the Child Tax Credit may be partially refundable depending on the year and jurisdiction.

## How Credits Work in Practice

Suppose you owe $3,000 in taxes and qualify for a $2,000 non-refundable credit:
- New tax bill: $3,000 − $2,000 = **$1,000**

If you owe $3,000 and qualify for a $4,000 refundable credit:
- New tax bill: $0
- Refund: **$1,000**

## Common Mistakes

### Assuming all credits are refundable

Always check whether a credit is refundable, non-refundable, or partially refundable before relying on it.

### Claiming credits you don't qualify for

Each credit has specific eligibility requirements. Claiming a credit you don't qualify for can result in penalties and interest.

## Key Takeaways

- Credits are more valuable than deductions because they reduce tax dollar-for-dollar.
- Refundable credits can result in a refund even if you don't owe taxes.
- Check eligibility requirements carefully for each credit.
- Keep documentation to support your credit claims.
