## What Is Tax Residency?

Tax residency is the legal determination of which country has the right to tax your worldwide income. It is distinct from citizenship or immigration status and is based on specific legal criteria.

## Why Tax Residency Matters

Your tax residency determines:
- **Which countries can tax your income**
- **What rates apply**
- **Which deductions and credits are available**
- **Whether you need to file a tax return**

## Common Tax Residency Tests

### 1. Physical Presence Test
Some countries determine residency based on the number of days you physically spend in the country. For example:
- **183 days or more** in a tax year often triggers residency in many jurisdictions
- Different countries use different thresholds

### 2. Permanent Home Test
If you have a permanent home available in a country, you may be considered a tax resident there — even if you don't spend most of your time there.

### 3. Center of Vital Interests
Some jurisdictions consider where you have your closest economic and personal ties — such as family, social ties, and asset locations.

### 4. habitual Residence
A cumulative test considering the number of days spent in each country over a period of years.

## The Tie-Breaker Rules

When two countries both consider you a tax resident, bilateral tax treaties provide **tie-breaker rules** to determine which country has the primary taxing right:

1. **Permanent home**: The country where you have a permanent home
2. **Center of vital interests**: The country with your closer personal and economic relations
3. **Habitual abode**: The country where you are physically present more
4. **Nationality**: The country of which you are a citizen (last resort)

## Residency for Different Situations

### Expatriates
If you live and work abroad, you may be a tax resident in your host country, your home country, or both. Understanding the rules helps you plan and potentially claim exemptions.

### Digital Nomads
Remote workers may establish residency unintentionally by spending extended periods in a single country. Planning is essential.

### Students
Students studying abroad often retain residency in their home country, but long stays can change this.

## What to Do If You Have Dual Residency

Dual residency is common when you have ties to multiple countries. In these situations:
- Review your tax treaty with both countries
- Consider filing in both jurisdictions with proper foreign tax credits
- Seek professional advice to optimize your position

<Warning>
Tax residency rules are complex and vary significantly between countries. This guide provides general educational information. Always consult a qualified tax professional for guidance specific to your situation.
</Warning>

## Key Takeaways

- Tax residency determines which country taxes your worldwide income.
- Residency is based on legal tests, not just where you live.
- Bilateral treaties prevent double taxation through tie-breaker rules.
- Multiple countries can consider you a tax resident simultaneously.
- Professional advice is essential when navigating dual residency.
