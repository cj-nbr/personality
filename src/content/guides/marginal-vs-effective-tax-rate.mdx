## Marginal Tax Rate

The **marginal tax rate** is the rate applied to your **last dollar** of income. In a progressive tax system, your marginal rate is the rate of the highest bracket your income falls into.

### Example

If your taxable income places you in the 24% bracket, your marginal rate is 24% — but only on the portion of income above that bracket's threshold.

## Effective Tax Rate

The **effective tax rate** is the **average** rate you actually pay across all your income. It is calculated as:

```
Effective Tax Rate = Total Tax ÷ Total Income
```

### Example

Suppose you have a taxable income of $85,000 and your total tax is $14,500:

```
Effective Rate = $14,500 ÷ $85,000 = 17.1%
```

Even though your marginal rate might be 22% or 24%, your effective rate is 17.1%.

## Why the Difference Matters

### Financial Planning

Understanding both rates helps you:
- **Estimate** the real tax impact of additional income
- **Evaluate** the value of deductions (a deduction saves you at your marginal rate)
- **Compare** tax situations across different income levels

### The Value of Deductions

A $1,000 deduction saves you more when you're in a 24% bracket ($240) than in a 12% bracket ($120). This is why high-income earners often focus on maximizing deductions.

## Common Mistakes

### Confusing marginal with effective

When someone says "I'm in the 32% bracket," they mean their marginal rate. Their effective rate is almost certainly lower.

### Ignoring state and local taxes

Your effective rate may be higher than the federal effective rate when you add state and local taxes.

## Summary

Your marginal rate determines the tax impact of one more dollar of income. Your effective rate tells you what percentage of your total income goes to taxes. Both are essential for smart tax planning.
