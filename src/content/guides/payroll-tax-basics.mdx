## What Is Payroll Tax?

Payroll taxes are taxes that employers and employees must pay based on employee wages and salaries. These taxes fund specific programs such as Social Security, Medicare, and unemployment insurance.

## Who Pays Payroll Taxes?

In most countries, payroll taxes are **shared between employers and employees**:

- **Employee portion**: Withheld from each paycheck
- **Employer portion**: Paid by the employer on top of the employee's wages

<Warning>
  Some payroll taxes, such as the employer's share of Social Security and Medicare in the United States, are not deducted from employee paychecks. They are an employer cost.
</Warning>

## Common Payroll Tax Types

### 1. Social Security Tax
Funds retirement, disability, and survivor benefits. In the United States, both employer and employee contribute a percentage of wages up to a wage base limit.

### 2. Medicare Tax
Funds the Medicare health insurance program for seniors. Unlike Social Security, there is generally no wage cap for Medicare tax. An additional Medicare tax may apply to high earners.

### 3. Unemployment Insurance
Funded by employers (in most jurisdictions), this provides temporary income to workers who lose their jobs through no fault of their own.

### 4. Other Payroll Taxes
Some jurisdictions impose additional payroll taxes for:
- Disability insurance
- Paid family leave
- State or local programs

## Payroll Tax vs Income Tax

| Aspect | Payroll Tax | Income Tax |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Funds specific programs (Social Security, Medicare) | General government revenue |
| **Rate structure** | Often fixed percentage up to a cap | Progressive (varies by income) |
| **Who pays** | Both employer and employee | Employee (employer may also pay) |
| **Cap** | Wage base limit on Social Security portion | No cap on income subject to tax |

## Calculating Payroll Tax

Payroll tax is typically calculated as a percentage of gross wages up to a statutory limit. For example:

```
Employee Social Security = Gross Wages × 6.2% (up to wage base)
Employee Medicare = Gross Wages × 1.45%
```

<Tip>
  Use a payroll tax calculator to account for wage base limits, additional Medicare tax, and employer portions accurately.
</Tip>

## Self-Employment Tax

Self-employed individuals must pay both the employer and employee portions of payroll taxes (Self-Employment Tax in the U.S.). However, the employer portion is deductible when calculating income tax.

## Key Takeaways

- Payroll taxes fund specific programs like Social Security and Medicare.
- Both employers and employees typically contribute.
- Payroll taxes are different from income tax in their purpose, structure, and caps.
- Self-employed individuals must cover both portions.
