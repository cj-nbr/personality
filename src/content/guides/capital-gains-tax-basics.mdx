## What Is Capital Gains Tax?

Capital gains tax is a tax on the **profit** you realize when you sell an asset that has increased in value. The asset could be stocks, real estate, bonds, cryptocurrency, or other investments.

Only the **gain** — the difference between your purchase price (cost basis) and your selling price — is taxed, not the total proceeds.

## Short-Term vs Long-Term Capital Gains

### Short-Term Capital Gains

Assets held for **one year or less** before selling are typically taxed at your **ordinary income tax rate**. This is generally a higher rate than long-term gains.

### Long-Term Capital Gains

Assets held for **more than one year** qualify for preferential long-term capital gains rates, which are typically lower than ordinary income rates.

| Holding Period | Tax Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1 year or less | Short-term (ordinary income rates) |
| More than 1 year | Long-term (preferential rates) |

<Example title="Short-Term vs Long-Term">
You buy 100 shares of stock for $10 each ($1,000 total). You sell them for $15 each ($1,500 total).

- **Gain**: $1,500 − $1,000 = $500
- If held less than 1 year: taxed as ordinary income (e.g., at 22%)
- If held more than 1 year: taxed at long-term capital gains rate (e.g., 15%)
</Example>

## Cost Basis

Your **cost basis** is what you paid for the asset, including:
- Purchase price
- Commissions and fees
- Improvements (for real estate)

Determining cost basis correctly is essential for calculating your actual gain.

## Strategies to Minimize Capital Gains Tax

### 1. Hold Assets Long-Term
Simply holding investments for more than one year can significantly reduce your tax bill.

### 2. Tax-Loss Harvesting
Selling investments at a loss can offset gains from other investments. Up to a certain amount of capital losses can offset ordinary income as well.

### 3. Use Tax-Advantaged Accounts
Investments held in retirement accounts (like IRAs or 401(k)s) may not trigger capital gains until withdrawal.

### 4. Gift or Donate Appreciated Assets
In some jurisdictions, donating appreciated assets to charity allows you to avoid capital gains tax entirely while receiving a charitable deduction.

## When Capital Gains Taxes Apply

Capital gains taxes typically apply to:
- **Stocks and bonds** sold for a profit
- **Real estate** (primary residence exclusions may apply)
- **Cryptocurrency** treated as property in many jurisdictions
- **Collectibles** (art, antiques, coins) taxed at special rates in some places

## Key Takeaways

- Capital gains tax applies to the profit from selling appreciating assets.
- Long-term gains generally enjoy lower rates than short-term gains.
- Holding investments for more than one year is the simplest strategy.
- Tax-loss harvesting can reduce your overall tax burden.
- Consult a tax professional for specific advice on your investment portfolio.
