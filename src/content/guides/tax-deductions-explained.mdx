## What Is a Tax Deduction?

A tax deduction is an amount that **reduces your taxable income**, which in turn lowers your overall tax bill. Deductions are one of the most powerful tools for managing your tax liability.

## How Deductions Work

### The Math

```
Taxable Income = Gross Income − Deductions − Exemptions
Tax = Taxable Income × Applicable Rates
```

A deduction of $1,000 at a 22% marginal rate saves you $220 in taxes. The higher your marginal rate, the more valuable each deduction.

### Standard Deduction vs. Itemized Deductions

Most tax systems offer two paths:

**Standard Deduction** — A fixed amount that reduces taxable income. It is simpler and available to most taxpayers.

**Itemized Deductions** — Individual deductions for specific expenses, such as:
- Mortgage interest
- State and local taxes (SALT)
- Charitable contributions
- Medical expenses exceeding a threshold

<Tip>
  Compare the standard deduction to your total itemized deductions. If your itemized total exceeds the standard deduction, itemizing will save you money.
</Tip>

## Common Types of Deductions

### 1. Business Expenses
Costs incurred in running a business, such as office supplies, travel, and equipment. Self-employed individuals can often deduct a wide range of business-related expenses.

### 2. Education Expenses
Tuition, fees, student loan interest, and education-related costs may qualify for deductions or credits depending on the jurisdiction.

### 3. Health Savings
Contributions to Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) or Medical Savings Accounts (MSAs) often provide an above-the-line deduction.

### 4. Retirement Contributions
Contributions to retirement accounts like 401(k)s or IRAs may be tax-deductible, reducing current taxable income while building long-term savings.

### 5. Moving Expenses
In some jurisdictions, expenses related to moving for a new job may be deductible (subject to specific rules).

## Deduction Stacking

When multiple deductions are available, the order in which they apply can matter. Some deductions are "above the line" (reduce gross income directly) while others are "below the line" (reduce taxable income after adjusted gross income is calculated).

## Deduction Limits and Phaseouts

Many deductions have:
- **Caps** — maximum deduction amounts
- **Phaseouts** - deduction amounts that reduce as income rises
- **Thresholds** - minimum expense amounts before a deduction kicks in

## Key Takeaways

- Deductions reduce taxable income, lowering your tax bill at your marginal rate.
- The standard deduction is simpler; itemizing may yield greater savings.
- Keep records of deductible expenses throughout the year.
- When in doubt, consult a tax professional for guidance specific to your situation.
