## What Is GST?

The **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** is a value-based consumption tax levied on most goods and services sold for domestic consumption. GST is charged at each stage of the supply chain but is ultimately borne by the end consumer.

### How GST Works

- **Registration**: Businesses above a turnover threshold must register for GST.
- **Charging GST**: Registered businesses charge GST on sales (output GST).
- **Claiming Input Tax Credit**: Businesses can claim credit for the GST they've paid on purchases (input GST).
- **Remittance**: The business remits the difference (output GST minus input GST) to the tax authority.

## What Is VAT?

**Value-Added Tax (VAT)** is similar to GST in concept — it is a consumption tax applied to the value added at each stage of production and distribution. VAT is used widely in Europe and many other parts of the world.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | GST | VAT |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Single-stage or multi-stage with input credit | Multi-stage with input credit |
| **Common Regions** | India, Australia, Canada, Singapore | European Union, many countries |
| **Rate Structure** | Often tiered (standard, reduced, zero) | Often single or dual rate |
| **Filing Frequency** | Typically monthly or quarterly | Typically monthly or quarterly |
| **Threshold** | Registration based on turnover threshold | Registration based on turnover threshold |

## Similarities

Both GST and VAT share these core principles:

1. **Destination-based**: Tax is collected where goods/services are consumed, not where they are produced.
2. **Multi-stage**: Tax applies at each stage of the supply chain.
3. **Input tax credit**: Businesses can reclaim the tax paid on inputs, avoiding cascading taxation.
4. **Consumer ultimately bears the cost**: Businesses act as collection agents.

<Warning title="Avoiding Cascading Taxation">
Without input tax credits, taxes would be applied on top of taxes at each stage — a phenomenon called "tax cascading." Both GST and VAT systems prevent this by allowing businesses to credit taxes paid at previous stages.
</Warning>

## GST/VAT Rates Around the World

| Country | Tax Type | Standard Rate |
|---|---|---|
| India | GST | 5%, 12%, 18%, 28% (tiered) |
| Australia | GST | 10% |
| Canada | GST/HST | 5% federal + provincial |
| Singapore | GST | 9% (as of 2024) |
| European Union | VAT | 15%–27% (varies by country) |
| United Kingdom | VAT | 20% (standard) |

## Practical Tips

- ** Businesses**: Register for GST/VAT if your revenue exceeds the threshold to remain compliant.
- ** Consumers**: GST/VAT is typically included in the displayed price in most countries.
- ** Businesses**: Use a GST/VAT calculator to quickly determine tax amounts on invoices and purchases.

## Summary

GST and VAT are consumption taxes that apply at each stage of production and distribution. While structurally similar, they differ in regional application, rate structures, and administrative requirements.
