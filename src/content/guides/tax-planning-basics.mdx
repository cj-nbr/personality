## What Is Tax Planning?

Tax planning is the **legal** analysis of a financial situation or plan to ensure that tax obligations are minimized. It involves arranging your finances so that you take advantage of all available deductions, credits, and other tax benefits.

## Tax Planning vs Tax Avoidance vs Tax Evasion

| Concept | Description | Legality |
|---|---|---|
| **Tax Planning** | Proactive arrangement of finances to reduce tax | Legal |
| **Tax Avoidance** | Using loopholes to reduce tax that was not intended | Legal (but controversial) |
| **Tax Evasion** | Illegally failing to pay taxes | Illegal |

Tax planning works within the spirit and letter of the law. It is a legitimate financial strategy.

## Core Tax Planning Strategies

### 1. Defer Income
If you expect to be in a lower tax bracket in the future, deferring income can reduce your current tax bill. Examples include:
- Delaying bonus payments to the next tax year
- Using retirement accounts that grow tax-deferred

### 2. Accelerate Deductions
Conversely, if you expect to be in a higher bracket next year, prepaying deductible expenses now maximizes their value.

### 3. Maximize Retirement Contributions
Contributions to tax-advantaged retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, etc.) reduce taxable income while building long-term savings.

### 4. Harvest Tax Losses
Selling investments at a loss to offset gains from other investments, reducing your overall capital gains tax.

### 5. Choose the Right Filing Status
Your filing status (single, married filing jointly, head of household, etc.) affects your tax brackets and eligibility for credits.

### 6. Utilize Tax Credits
Credits are more valuable than deductions. Prioritize claiming all credits you're eligible for.

## Timing Considerations

Tax planning is a year-round activity, but certain decisions matter based on timing:

- **Year-end**: Review deductions, capital gains/losses, and retirement contributions
- **Quarterly**: Estimated tax payments for self-employed individuals
- **Major life events**: Marriage, childbirth, home purchase, job change — all affect tax planning

## Common Tax Planning Mistakes

### Focusing only on April deductions
Tax planning should be continuous, not just a year-end scramble.

### Ignoring state and local taxes
Federal deductions may be offset by higher state obligations depending on your situation.

### Overlooking small deductions
Even small deductions add up over time. Keep records all year.

## Working with a Professional

While basic tax planning can be done independently, complex situations — such as self-employment, investment portfolios, or international income — benefit from professional guidance.

## Key Takeaways

- Tax planning is the legal, proactive minimization of tax obligations.
- Strategies include deferring income, accelerating deductions, and maximizing credits.
- Effective planning is a year-round effort, not a once-a-year activity.
- Simple strategies like retirement contributions and tax-loss harvesting benefit most taxpayers.
